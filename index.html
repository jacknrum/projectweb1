Our Duty: From Alarm to Ash

Long ago, firemen had to put fires out. Today every house is fireproof, so we've taken on a nobler job: keeping the public safe from confusion, contradiction, and the gloom that pages bring.

1. The Alarm

A concerned neighbor files a report. The firehouse bell rings, and the brass pole gets its exercise. Response time: faster than doubt.

2. The Response

The Salamander roars through the streets, siren singing, while our trusty hound sniffs out every nook and cranny.

3. The Burn

Kerosene, a flick of the igniter, and the pages curl up like black butterflies. Fast, clean, and beautiful to watch.

4. The Calm

Neighbors return to their parlor walls and radios. The worry is gone, the smoke clears, and the block goes back to being pleasant.

Know the Signs of a Book Hoarder

  • Asks "why" instead of "how." Good citizens want to know how things work, not why.
  • Walks at night, on purpose. Especially without a destination or a car.
  • Leaves the parlor walls switched off. A dark wall is a busy mind.
  • Talks about the past fondly. Or mentions things "they read somewhere."
  • Smells faintly of old paper. Our hound notices. So should you.
  • Seems quietly thoughtful. Happy people don't need to stare at rain.
